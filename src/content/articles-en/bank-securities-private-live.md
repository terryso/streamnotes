Banks, securities firms, and asset managers increasingly use live streaming to talk to clients: relationship managers discuss the markets in owned channels, brokerages run investor education series, and head offices train their branches. Compared with e-commerce livestreams, the hard part in finance is not whether you can stream, but what you say, who can watch, and what happens when someone misspeaks. This article lays out the scenarios and compliance framework for private-domain (owned-channel) finance livestreams, gives a before/during/after checklist, and then uses POLYV's published finance solution to show how selection and rollout can work.

> Private-domain finance livestreams are not about traffic. They are about the right people, at the right entry point, seeing compliant content.

## 1. Separate the three scenarios

| Scenario | Typical content | Main audience | Compliance and platform focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Client marketing | Wealth seminars, product introductions, perks | Existing and prospective clients | Script review, risk disclosure, interaction and link control |
| Investor education | Market commentary, basics, risk warnings | Ordinary investors | Separating education from marketing, no definitive predictions |
| Internal training | Policy briefings, product and compliance training | Employees, branches | Identity authentication, tiered permissions, no leaks |

One platform can serve all three, but configure them separately. Marketing streams face clients and need strong interaction and conversion features; training streams face employees and care most about authentication and permission isolation. Mixing them in one channel hurts both compliance and analytics.

## 2. The compliance framework: three documents to know

Finance livestreams touch many rules, and your institution's compliance department sets the final interpretation. These three public documents are a starting point for the baseline:

### 1. Measures for the Administration of Online Livestream Marketing (Trial)

Issued by the Cyberspace Administration of China and six other departments, effective May 25, 2021. It sets clear requirements for live room operators and livestream marketers, such as publishing product or service information truthfully, accurately, and completely, not publishing false or misleading information, and not fabricating or tampering with view or like counts. For institutions, links and QR codes inside the live room should also fall under information security management.

### 2. Measures for the Suitability Management of Securities and Futures Investors

These measures divide investors into ordinary and professional investors, and ordinary investors receive special protection in information disclosure, risk warnings, and suitability matching. Firms may not give investors definitive judgments about uncertain matters, and may not actively pitch products whose risk level exceeds an ordinary investor's risk tolerance. On a livestream, that means presenters cannot say things like "guaranteed returns" or "principal protected," and must disclose risks whenever a specific product comes up.

### 3. Personal Information Protection Law

The law lists financial account information as sensitive personal information, which may be processed only for a specific purpose, with sufficient necessity, and under strict protection. Owned-channel livestreams often identify clients and record viewing behavior, so disclose the processing rules at the entry point, collect only what is necessary, and restrict back-office export permissions.

## 3. Before, during, and after: an operations checklist

**Before the stream**

- Send scripts and slides through compliance review, checking product information, return statements, and risk warnings item by item.
- Confirm presenters' professional qualifications and on-camera authorization.
- Set viewing permissions: open marketing streams by client segment, and restrict training streams to authenticated employees.
- Review product cards, links, QR codes, and ad slots in the live room in advance.

**During the stream**

- Turn on comment moderation or keyword filtering and assign someone to monitor in real time.
- Delete and mute stock tips, traffic diversion, and abusive messages promptly.
- Prepare contingency plans: how to correct a presenter's slip, and how to switch to a backup stream if the network drops.
- For important streams, staff two people: one for content, one for interaction and compliance.

**After the stream**

- Review the replay again before publishing, and edit out slips if needed.
- Keep stream, interaction, and review records so everything can be traced later.
- Export viewing data and map it to client segments in the institution's CRM.

## 4. Client engagement: put the stream inside your owned funnel

The value of private-domain streaming is knowing who your viewers are. A few practical moves:

1. **One entry point**: Embed the stream in the mobile banking or brokerage app, official account, or mini program, so clients don't need to install anything and can be identified by their institution account.
2. **Client segmentation**: Configure content and viewing rights by asset tier, risk assessment result, or assigned relationship manager.
3. **Head office and branch coordination**: Head office plans the content themes and compliance wording; branches run topical sessions for their local clients.
4. **Follow-up**: Route high-intent clients to relationship managers based on watch time, questions, and interaction, and keep follow-up scripts just as compliant.

## Conclusion

Private-domain finance livestreams work when scenario separation, end-to-end compliance, and client engagement move forward together. Running the process end to end with a small training or investor education session first, then expanding to marketing, is safer than starting with a big event.
